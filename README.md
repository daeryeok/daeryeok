## Operating Rules

1. **Give each project one accountable human owner.** Keep authority over architecture, method, schedule, and release in the same hands as responsibility.

2. **Keep working groups small and capable.** Give human collaborators and software agents explicit roles, boundaries, and decision rights.

3. **Restrict access to unfinished or sensitive work.** Share by need, protect deep work, and grant every person, agent, and service the minimum practical access.

4. **Define the work before building it.** Agree on requirements, success criteria, interfaces, constraints, and decision rights first. Ambiguity discovered early is inexpensive; ambiguity institutionalized is not.

5. **Keep systems simple, modular, inspectable, and reversible.** Admit complexity only while it keeps justifying its operational cost to the owner.

6. **Keep reporting lean and documentation thorough.** Record assumptions, decisions, tests, failures, interfaces, and reasons for change—not status theater.

7. **Review time, cost, risk, and technical debt using current evidence.** Avoid stale assumptions, hidden commitments, and surprise overruns.

8. **Choose tools, vendors, automation, and collaborators for competence, fit, and incentives.** Institutional habit is not a technical requirement.

9. **Build quality in at the source.** Reject invalid inputs early, verify critical dependencies, and never pay twice for the same defect.

10. **Test prototypes early under real conditions.** A team that cannot test what it builds will eventually lose the ability to design it well.

11. **Make the work reproducible.** Document reasoning, constraints, interfaces, validation results, and known limits so another competent maintainer—or a well-scoped agent—can continue it.

12. **Use direct communication and explicit handoffs.** Trust between people and clear interfaces for automation prevent more failure than unnecessary process layers.

13. **Release publicly only after verification.** Scope claims to evidence, state limitations plainly, and own finished work without unnecessarily exposing private process.

14. **Measure performance by impact, reliability, judgment, and learning speed—not team size, title, meeting count, or visibility.**

This is Daeryeok’s operating standard, loosely adapted from Clarence “Kelly” Johnson’s
[14 Rules and Practices](https://www.lockheedmartin.com/content/dam/lockheed-martin/aero/photo/skunkworks/kellys-14-rules.pdf)
developed for Lockheed Martin’s
[Skunk Works®](https://www.lockheedmartin.com/en-us/who-we-are/business-areas/aeronautics/skunkworks/skunk-works-origin-story.html).
